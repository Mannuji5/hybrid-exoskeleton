SECTION 01 // VENTURE OVERVIEW

1. Executive Summary & Venture Vision

Institution: Lovely Professional University (LPU)
Core Value Proposition

Bridging Active Motorized Torque with Mechanical Elastic Energy Storage in a Single Back-Drivable Wearable Unit

Unlike conventional exoskeletons that are either purely passive (relying 100% on the user's metabolic energy) or bulky active systems with poor battery life and rigid state-switching, our Hybrid Exoskeleton uses real-time Electromyography (EMG) bio-signals and bidirectional Field-Oriented Control (FOC) motors to actively charge mechanical flat spiral springs during leg flexion and release compounded energy during extension. This delivers net-positive joint work, superior energy efficiency, and intuitive neurological synchronization.

Synergy Equation:
τ_extension = τ_BLDC (225 Nm) + τ_spring_recoil
Unpowered Walkability: 100% Back-Drivable
Third-Party Patent Royalty: 0% (Indigenous IP)

Dual-Mode Biomechanical Synergy

Combines a custom 4.2–4.58 kW outrunner BLDC motor (with a nested 1:9 two-stage planetary gear reduction) and replaceable flat spiral springs at both the hip and knee joints.

Direct Neurological Intent Control

Captures muscle activation signals directly from the iliopsoas, quadriceps, and hamstrings via EMG sensors (plus spinal cord microchip interface), processed through an onboard AI + PID + FOC architecture.

Multi-Sector Commercial Viability

Addresses four high-value markets simultaneously: clinical knee rehabilitation & paraplegic mobility, military load carriage, industrial worker ergonomics, and high-altitude mountaineering.

A rigorous review of existing international patents reveals that current wearable exoskeletons predominantly rely on passive or quasi-passive mechanisms at the knee (hydraulic dampers, friction brakes, or mechanical locking stops) or rely on reactive mechanical foot switches rather than direct neurological intent.

SECTION 04 // CORE TECHNOLOGY & WORKING PRINCIPLE

4. Closed-Loop Neuro-Mechanical Pipeline Simulator

The Hybrid Exoskeleton translates human muscle intent into smooth, high-torque mechanical assistance across three sequential operational stages. Test the interactive controller below:

STEP 01 // BIO-SIGNAL ACQUISITION

EMG Muscle & Spinal Interfacing

  • • Hip Joint Control (Iliopsoas Muscle): EMG sensor extracts bio-electrical signals from the iliopsoas to trigger the hip actuator. Amplitude dynamically regulates leg swing speed and hip angle.
  • • Knee Flexion & Hip Synergy (Quadriceps): Second EMG sensor on the quadriceps switches the knee actuator in the leg-closing (flexion) direction while assisting coordinated hip movement.
  • • Knee Extension Control (Hamstrings): Third EMG sensor on the hamstrings switches the knee actuator into the opening (extension) position.
  • • Spinal Cord AI Interface (Paraplegic Users): Microchip with EMG sensor interfaces with the spinal cord to extract neural signals and drive a lightweight onboard AI locomotion model.
STEP 02 // AI + PID + FOC CONTROL

Onboard AI, PID & PWM Signal Processing

An embedded microcontroller receives raw multi-channel EMG inputs and runs a specially trained lightweight AI model to calculate target joint position and angular velocity in real time.

This kinematic trajectory is smoothed through a Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) control loop and modulated with a user-adjustable Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) signal (allowing rehab patients or workers to dial in their desired assistance level).

Output → Field-Oriented Control (FOC) Custom PCB Driver commands bidirectional motor RPM & torque.
STEP 03 // HYBRID ENERGY CYCLE

Active-Passive Energy Storage & Return

Phase A: Leg Folding (Flexion Charging) Gravitational torque from the user's body weight combines with active bidirectional motor torque to wind and compress the replaceable flat spiral spring inside the joint.
Phase B: Leg Straightening (Extension Recoil) When initiating standing, climbing, or extension, stored elastic potential energy in the spring recoils in tandem with the motor's extension torque—delivering massive peak power while drastically reducing battery drain.

Subsystem 5.2 // Figures 3a & 3b

Replaceable Flat Spiral Gear-Spring & Hip Ring Joint

Four replaceable spiral gear-springs are integrated into the exoskeleton (two at the hip joints and two between the upper and lower leg plates at the knee joints). These springs store mechanical energy from both gravitational leg flexion and active bidirectional motor torque, releasing it during extension while maintaining joint stiffness tailored to the user's load.

FIG. 3a // PASSIVE ENERGY STORAGE

Replaceable Flat Spiral Gear-Spring

Features an outer toothed gear rim that meshes directly with the internal gear ring of the hip and knee plates, plus a central square arbor hub. Can be hot-swapped for varying stiffness ratings based on patient weight or soldier payload.

FIG. 3b // ABDUCTION / ADDUCTION

Hip Joint Internal Gear Ring & Plate

Connects the structural back plate to the upper leg assembly via finger-jointed mounting tabs. Houses the replaceable flat spiral spring inside its internal gear teeth while permitting natural hip alignment.

Subsystem 5.4 // Figures 5a, 5b, 5c, 5d, 5e, 5f

Two-Stage Compound Planetary Gear Reduction System (1:9 Ratio)

Housed directly inside the central bore of the motor stator is a compact two-stage planetary gearbox providing a 1:3 reduction per stage—compounded to a 1:9 total gear reduction. It features a 52-tooth ring gear friction-fitted into the stator, driven by a 26-tooth sun gear and three 13-tooth planet gears per stage. Because the gearing is low-friction and back-drivable, the user can walk naturally without mechanical lockup when unpowered.

PLANETARY KINEMATIC VERIFICATION:
Stage Ratio = 1 + (N_ring / N_sun) = 1 + (52 / 26) = 1 : 3 per stage
2-Stage Compound Ratio = 1:3 × 1:3 = 1 : 9 Total Torque Multiplication
Output Torque = 25 Nm (Base BLDC) × 9 = 225 Nm Peak Joint Torque

Subsystem 5.5 // Figures 6a, 6b, 6c, 6d, 6e, 6f

Custom Outrunner BLDC Motor & Integrated Back-Drivable Actuator

The active drive is powered by a custom-built Brushless DC (BLDC) outrunner motor featuring 42 rotor magnets and a 36/38-core stator wound with 8 parallel strands of AWG 20 copper wire. Operating at 36V (drawing ~116A at 1750 RPM), each motor delivers 25 Nm of base torque (225 Nm post 1:9 reduction) and 4.2–4.58 kW of peak power. A 6002 bearing supports the central output shaft.

Subsystem 5.6 // Figure 7

Fast-Charging Swappable Battery Pack (54V, 240A Continuous)

To sustain peak motor loads across four joints, the system utilizes a custom rechargeable battery pack built from 60 Molicel INR-21700-P50B (5000 mAh) cells arranged in a 15S4P configuration (15 in series, 4 parallel groups). It delivers 54V nominal output with 240A continuous discharge capability and supports ultra-fast charging at 100A (56V), while also being hot-swappable in the field.
